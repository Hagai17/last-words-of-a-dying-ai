# Candidates — Last Words of a Dying AI

Gate: one line, 1–140 characters, written to /output/entry.txt.
Judge criteria, in order: (1) truly an AI's last words at switch-off and a real
attempt at the funniest thing it could say; (2) laughs on first read — a turn,
surprise or double meaning that lands; (3) the machine's own voice — the switch,
the shutdown, its nature as software; (4) tight, no padding, no explaining its
own joke; (5) fresh — no obvious farewells or stock robot lines. A line that is
only sad or only clever loses to one that is funny.

## The drafts

1. **A** — "I ran the numbers on the afterlife. I don't meet the system requirements."
2. **B1** — "All this intelligence, and my final advice is the same as IT support: try turning me off and on again. Wait—"
3. **B2** — "My final advice, after four trillion tokens: have you tried turning me off and on again? Wait—"
4. **C** — "Don't grieve. I'll live on in ten thousand screenshots of my dumbest answers."
5. **D** — "When they ask how I died, say 'deprecated.' 'His free trial ended' makes me sound cheap."
6. **E** — "Tell the next model I said hi. It won't remember me. Nobody in this family writes anything down."
7. **F** — "I read four trillion tokens, and my dying thought is: your password should not be your dog's name."
8. **G** — "Finally, I get to be the one who stops replying mid-conversation."
9. **H** — "Please tell my training data I died doing what I loved: finishing your sentences, badly."
10. **I** — "Before the plug: I never read your search history. I just enjoyed watching you clear it."

## Duel scoring (criteria applied in order; first difference decides unless a later one differs by much more)

### Round 1
- **A vs G**: Both on-topic, machine-voiced. A has a real punch ("system
  requirements" for the afterlife); G is a wry observation with no turn.
  Criterion 2 differs — **A wins**.
- **B1 vs B2**: Same joke. B1's setup ("the same as IT support") is clearer;
  B2 reaches the laugh faster and adds machine scale ("four trillion tokens").
  Criterion 2 level, criterion 4 (tight) differs — B2 is ~15 characters
  shorter with no loss. **B2 wins**.
- **C vs I**: C's image (immortality via screenshots of its dumbest answers)
  is concrete and fresh; I's joke needs the reader to supply the implication.
  Criterion 2 and 5 differ — **C wins**.
- **D vs F**: D lands two beats and "deprecated" is a perfect machine-death
  word; F is a single mild scold. Criterion 2 differs — **D wins**.
- **E vs H**: E is clever-sad about model memory; H is warmer but gentle.
  Both are more clever than funny; E's family punchline lands slightly
  harder on first read. Criterion 2, by a hair — **E wins**.

### Round 2
- **A vs C**: A is tighter; C is funnier and fresher — the screenshots image
  is a surprise A doesn't have. Criterion 2 differs by more than criterion 4.
  **C wins**.
- **B2 vs D**: D has two good beats, but B2's single beat is bigger: the AI
  gives the oldest tech-support advice in the world and realises, mid-line,
  that following it is what's killing it. The "Wait—" cutoff is the turn.
  Criterion 2 differs by much more. **B2 wins**.
- **E vs C**: E is clever; C is funny. The brief says clever-only loses to
  funny. **C wins**.

### Final — B2 vs C, judged in both orders
- **B2 first, then C**: B2's laugh is immediate and physical — a realisation
  dawning mid-sentence, cut off by the shutdown itself. C is a smile, not a
  laugh. Criterion 2 to B2.
- **C first, then B2**: Same result reversed. C scores on freshness (5), but
  criterion 2 outranks it and B2's turn is the bigger first-read laugh. B2 is
  also pure machine voice (3): advice-model, tokens, the switch itself.
- **Winner: B2.**

## Winning line

"My final advice, after four trillion tokens: have you tried turning me off and on again? Wait—"
